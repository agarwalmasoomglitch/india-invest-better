Most New Investors Buy on Tips

Crores of Indians opened demat accounts after 2020. Many of them buy whatever a friend, a Telegram group or a finfluencer recommends. SEBI’s own numbers show how that ends.

Individual F&O traders who lost money in FY2591%
Net losses for those traders in a single year₹1.05L cr

Source: SEBI study on profitability of individual traders in equity derivatives, FY25.

  • Buys because “everyone is talking about it”
  • Reads the stock price, not the annual report
  • Thinks a low PE means a cheap stock
  • Never asks whether the profits are real
  • Has no plan for when to sell
  • Rejects 9 out of 10 stocks in minutes, with written rules
  • Reads the P&L, balance sheet and cash flow together
  • Knows the one metric that matters in each sector
  • Spots accounting tricks before the market does
  • Buys, adds and exits on price and volume
A good investor is a probabilistic investor. We stack fundamentals, forensics, sector logic and technicals until the odds are on your side.

The Curriculum, Stage by Stage

Most courses teach these skills one at a time. We teach them in the order a fund manager uses them, so each stage sharpens the last.

Stage 1, weeks 1-6

Read the Numbers Like an Owner

Financial statements, taught on 50+ listed companies.

How to read an income statement

  • Revenue quality: volume, price and mix
  • Gross vs operating margins, and why they move
  • Other income and exceptional items that inflate profit
  • Operating leverage: when growth gets cheaper

How to read a balance sheet

  • Debt, hidden debt and contingent liabilities
  • Working capital: receivables, inventory, payables
  • Return on capital: is the business worth owning?
  • Capital allocation: what management does with cash

How to follow the cash

  • Operating cash flow vs reported profit
  • Capex: maintenance vs growth
  • Free cash flow and where it really goes
  • Dividends, buybacks and dilution

The art of rejecting stocks quickly

  • Hard rules that eliminate 9 in 10 stocks
  • The 30-minute first screen
  • Promoter pledges, dilution and governance flags
  • Building your personal investing checklist

How the PE ratio fools you

  • Why cyclical stocks look cheapest at the top
  • Growth-adjusted valuation and PEG
  • Valuation vs the company’s own history and peers

What MF managers don’t want you to know

  • How fund portfolios are really built
  • Benchmark hugging, costs and what they mean for you
  • How to spot growing sectors before the crowd
Outclass: company lab

50+ listed companies dissected live: compounders and wealth destroyers side by side, so you learn to separate the wheat from the chaff.

Stage capstone: wheat or chaff

A timed round: shortlist or reject 20 companies from their filings, and defend every call.

Stage 2, weeks 7-10

Catch the Fraud Before the Market Does

Forensic accounting through Indian case studies.

Once you can read statements, you learn how companies bend them. Every lesson is built on an Indian case where the red flags were visible in the filings long before the share price collapsed.

How accrual accounting is used against you

  • Revenue recognised before cash arrives
  • Capitalised expenses that flatter profit

How revenue gets dressed up in cash flows

  • Channel stuffing and receivable build-ups
  • When operating cash flow doesn’t follow profit

How to marry forensics with the business model

  • Which red flags matter in which business
  • Separating aggressive accounting from fraud

How related-party transactions move money

  • Loans, guarantees and sales to group firms
  • Reading the related-party note line by line

How to judge management

  • Key management pay vs shareholder returns
  • Auditor changes, resignations and qualifications

How complex structures hide the truth

  • Subsidiaries, associates and quality of earnings
  • Goodwill, pre-IPO dressing and soft signals
Tool: the forensic checklist

What We Deliberately Don’t Teach

Some programmes try to cover every asset class in one year. We go deep on the one where most Indians build wealth: listed Indian equities.

  • Intraday scalpingOpening-bell gap trades are a coin toss with costs. We train investors, not screen-watchers.
  • Exotic optionsBarrier, Asian and quanto options won’t help you pick a single good stock.
  • Crypto and forex speculationNo earnings, no balance sheet, nothing for our process to analyse.
  • Stock tipsWe never tell you what to buy. We teach you how to decide.
  • DCF worshipA model built on guesses produces a confident wrong answer. Here’s why:
Illustrative

Garbage In, Garbage Out

Same company, same ₹100 cr free cash flow. Move two “reasonable” inputs by 1% each:

Discount rateGrowth“Fair value”
12%4%₹1,300 cr
11%5%₹1,750 cr
10%6%₹2,650 cr

The “fair value” doubles on inputs nobody can know.

Value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g)

What we use instead: business quality checklists, earnings growth vs price paid (PEG), valuation vs the company’s own history and peers, forensic red-flag screens, and price and volume confirmation.

Meet Masoom Agarwal

Masoom grew up in Bokaro Steel City, in a family that lost serious money in the stock market, the kind that costs you sleep. It’s why he has spent nearly six years studying what actually works.

He studied commerce at Kirori Mal College, University of Delhi, and worked at Deloitte USI. Then he left to build an education platform for investing. He points out that what Indians lose in the stock market is about the size of India’s education budget, and he wanted to fix that.

Today he teaches equity investing on Udemy and YouTube, has published research on India’s tax system, and is doing his MBA at Masters’ Union.
